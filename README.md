# Why Continuous Assurance Did Not Happen Until Now

The economics of cognition, the limits of GRC software, and what AI actually changes.

Read it: **https://rmhrisk.github.io/continuous-assurance/**

## What this is

A sequel to [The Assurance Model Was Built for a World That No Longer Exists](https://rmhrisk.github.io/assurance-model/). That post argued periodic assurance is losing its epistemic reach. This one asks the follow-up question the diagnosis leaves open: if the alternative has been imaginable for decades, why did nobody build it?

The answer is not that the profession was slow. It was responding rationally to a cost structure. For thirty years software reduced the administrative cost of compliance without materially reducing the cognitive cost — it could store, route, collect, and report, but not interpret, adjudicate, challenge, or learn from experience.

Four parts:

1. **Why Not Sooner** — three generations of GRC software, the adoption reality, and four constraints of which AI moves only two
2. **The Economics of Cognition** — knowledge versus intelligence versus experience, tacit knowledge as the missing corpus, and what happens when an argument costs nothing
3. **What Continuous Requires** — policy versus practice, compliance as a falsifiable hypothesis, the signal-to-assurance ladder, and reasoning rather than monitoring
4. **What It Inherits** — representation dependence, Goodhart at machine speed, over-inference, and why the system must be contestable by design

## Structure

Single self-contained `index.html`. No build step, no dependencies, no analytics. Diagrams are hand-authored inline SVG driven by CSS custom properties; the only external request is to Google Fonts.
